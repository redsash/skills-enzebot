# Spatial UX & Circulation Design Guide

Ergonomic standards, circulation hierarchy, and acoustic/visual privacy guidelines for architectural floor plans.

---

## 1. Circulation Hierarchy & Minimum Clearances

Corridors and pathways must be sized according to anticipated traffic density:

| Traffic Tier | Typical Zones Connected | Minimum Clear Width | Recommended Clear Width |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Arterial Spines** (High) | Entry $\leftrightarrow$ Living $\leftrightarrow$ Kitchen $\leftrightarrow$ Main Terrace | **42 inches** | **48 to 60 inches** (allows two adults to comfortably pass) |
| **Secondary Corridors** (Medium) | Hallways to Bedrooms, Guest Suites, Bathrooms | **36 inches** | **42 inches** |
| **Service Paths** (Low) | Laundry $\leftrightarrow$ Mechanical Core $\leftrightarrow$ Cistern / Storage | **32 inches** | **36 inches** |
| **Kitchen Work Aisles** | Counter to Island / Prep to Cooking / Refrigerator clearance | **42 inches** (single cook) | **48 inches** (multiple cooks) |

---

## 2. Door Swings & Collision Prevention

- **Inward Swing Rule:** Interior room doors must swing inward into the destination room and rest flat against the nearest adjacent perpendicular wall.
- **Corridor Intrusion:** Doors must never swing 90 degrees outward into an arterial or secondary hallway where they create collision traps.
- **Opposing Doors:** When two doors are in close proximity (e.g., bedroom and hall powder room), maintain at least 36" between hinge/latch jambs to prevent interlocking door leaves.

---

## 3. Acoustic Buffering & Zoning

Noise-generating zones must be separated from quiet resting areas:
- **Buffer Layering:** Never place a bedroom headboard on a shared partition with:
  - Dishwashers, kitchen prep counters, or refrigerators.
  - Mechanical rooms housing inverters, lithium batteries, or automated booster pumps.
  - Home theater or media soundbars.
- **Buffer Strategies:**
  - Place walk-in closets, ensuite bathrooms, or circulation hallways between living rooms and bedroom sleeping spaces.
  - Use continuous 8-inch solid-grouted CMU shear walls or double-stud staggered acoustic partitions between public and private wings.

---

## 4. Sightline & Visual Privacy

- **Front Door Baffle:** When standing at the main entryway door, visitors should see into inviting public spaces (living, garden, view corridor), but never have a direct axial sightline into:
  - Private bedroom doors or unmade beds.
  - Open bathrooms or powder room water closets.
- **Powder Room Baffle:** Powder rooms should feature an offset vestibule or 90-degree turn so guests can exit discreetly without facing the dining table or kitchen seating.

---

## 5. Transit Flow Optimization

1. **Grocery & Logistics Transit:** The path from vehicle parking/entry to the kitchen pantry should be direct ($\le 2$ transit hops), with no narrow pinch points or awkward 180-degree hairpin turns.
2. **Beach & Pool (Wet-to-Dry) Transit:** For coastal and resort homes, occupants returning from swimming should have direct access to an outdoor shower, powder room, or laundry area without tracking sand or pool water across dry hardwood or tile living areas.
